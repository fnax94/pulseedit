Your camera speaks log. Pulse Color is fluent in 18 dialects.

Pick your camera, pick a look, done. Every profile is built from measured color science — not eyeballed LUTs:

  • Sony — S-Log3 & S-Log2, every Color Mode the camera menu offers (S-Gamut3.Cine, S-Gamut3, S-Gamut, BT.2020, 709, Venice)
  • ARRI — LogC3 / Wide Gamut 3, LogC4 / Wide Gamut 4
  • Canon — Canon Log 3 & Log 2 with Cinema Gamut, BT.2020 or BT.709
  • Apple — Apple Log and Apple Log 2 (iPhone 15–17 Pro), Rec.2020 / P3-D65 / Rec.709
  • Panasonic V-Log · Fujifilm F-Log, F-Log2, F-Log2 C · RED Log3G10 · Nikon N-Log · DJI D-Log · Leica L-Log
  • No log? No problem. Rec.709 and sRGB inputs are built in — graded footage, older cameras and phone SDR work too (and it's the default).

Six looks. Zero fake film grain by default.

Nordic · Cinema Print · Midnight · Daylight · Linen · Amber — print-film rendering chains (cross-talk, highlight desaturation, palette shaping), not Instagram filters. A Look Strength dial blends each one from subtle to full. Clean by design: film character comes from curve, color and light — grain is there if you want it, off if you don't.

The filter drawer — stackable, like real glass

Twelve optical FX, each with its own on/off toggle, freely combinable — the way DPs stack filters on set:

  • Black Pro-Mist · White Pro-Mist · Glimmerglass · Skin Diffusion — the diffusion family, with sliders graded like the physical filters (0.125 = 1/8, 0.25 = 1/4, 0.5 = 1/2, 1 = full)
  • Halation · Bloom · Anamorphic Streak · Star Filter (4/6/8 points) — light effects measured to behave like the real thing
  • Enhance · Chromatic Aberration · Vignette · Film Grain — finishing touches, all opt-in

Everything runs in real time on a 25p HD timeline (LUT-accelerated engine, all CPU cores, no GPU requirements).
